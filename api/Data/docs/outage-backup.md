# Backup Power During Outages

## What happens when the grid goes down

When the grid goes down, the VoltNest 13 battery switches your home to backup power automatically, in less than a second. Most people never notice the switch: lights stay on and devices keep running. During an outage, the VoltNest 13 can use its entire stored charge, including the reserve percentage it normally holds back, down to a 5% floor that protects the battery. When grid power returns, the battery reconnects on its own and goes back to your chosen operating mode.

## How long backup lasts

How long the VoltNest 13 can power your home in an outage depends on how much charge it has and how much power your home is using. A good estimate is: hours of backup ≈ (state of charge % − 5%) × 13.5 kWh ÷ your home's load in kW. The 5% is the floor the battery keeps to protect itself. For example, at 42% charge with the home using 1.2 kW, the battery holds about (0.37 × 13.5) ≈ 5.0 usable kWh, which lasts about 5.0 ÷ 1.2 ≈ 4.2 hours. The current load is the power shown on the battery status screen while it is discharging.

## Stretching backup time

You can make the VoltNest 13 last longer during an outage by lowering your home's load. Turn off the largest users first: air conditioning, electric ovens and stoves, clothes dryers, pool pumps, and electric vehicle chargers. Cutting the load in half roughly doubles the backup time. During daylight, rooftop solar keeps recharging the battery during an outage, so a sunny day can extend backup well beyond the estimate from the battery's charge alone.

## Preparing for a storm

If a storm or planned outage is coming, prepare the VoltNest 13 the day before. Switch the battery to Backup Only mode, which keeps it full and saves its energy for the outage, or raise the reserve percentage to 80% or higher in the app. Charge the battery fully if you can, using solar during the day or the grid overnight in Time-Based Control mode. After the storm passes, switch back to your usual mode.

## What backup can't power

The VoltNest 13 supplies up to 5.0 kW at a time, so during an outage it cannot run large loads that add up to more than 5.0 kW, such as central air conditioning together with an electric oven and dryer. It also only powers circuits connected to the backup panel; anything wired outside it goes dark in an outage. Changing which circuits are backed up requires work in the electrical panel and must be done by a licensed electrician.
