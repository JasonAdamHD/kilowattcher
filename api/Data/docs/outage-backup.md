# Backup Power During Outages

## What happens when the grid goes down

When the grid goes down, your home battery switches your home to backup power automatically, in less than a second. Most people never notice the switch: lights stay on and devices keep running. During an outage, the battery can use its entire stored charge, including the reserve percentage it normally holds back, down to a small backup floor that protects the battery. When grid power returns, the battery reconnects on its own and goes back to your chosen operating mode.

## How long backup lasts

How long your battery can power your home in an outage depends on how much charge it has, its capacity, and how much power your home is using. A good estimate is: hours of backup ≈ (state of charge % − backup floor %) × capacity in kWh ÷ your home's load in kW. The backup floor is 5% for the VoltNest 13 and 10% for the Helio PowerCell 10; your battery's manual lists it. For example, a VoltNest 13 (13.5 kWh) at 42% charge with the home using 1.2 kW holds about (0.37 × 13.5) ≈ 5.0 usable kWh, which lasts about 4.2 hours.

## Stretching backup time

You can make your battery last longer during an outage by lowering your home's load. Turn off the largest users first: air conditioning, electric ovens and stoves, clothes dryers, pool pumps, and electric vehicle chargers. Cutting the load in half roughly doubles the backup time. During daylight, rooftop solar keeps recharging the battery during an outage, so a sunny day can extend backup well beyond the estimate from the battery's charge alone.

## Preparing for a storm

If a storm or planned outage is coming, prepare your battery the day before. Switch it to its outage-saving mode, which keeps it full for the outage, or raise the reserve percentage to 80% or higher in the app. On a VoltNest 13 that mode is called Backup Only; on a Helio PowerCell 10 it is called Storm Guard. Charge the battery fully if you can, using solar during the day or the grid overnight. After the storm passes, switch back to your usual mode.

## What backup can't power

Your battery can only supply so much power at once: up to 5.0 kW for a VoltNest 13 and 3.8 kW for a Helio PowerCell 10. During an outage it cannot run large loads that add up to more than that, such as central air conditioning together with an electric oven and dryer. It also only powers circuits connected to the backup panel; anything wired outside it goes dark in an outage. Changing which circuits are backed up requires work in the electrical panel and must be done by a licensed electrician.
