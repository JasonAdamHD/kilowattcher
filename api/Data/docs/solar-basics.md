# Your Rooftop Solar System

## System size

Your rooftop solar system is rated at 7.2 kW. It is made up of eighteen 400 W panels on the roof, connected to an inverter that turns the panels' power into electricity your home can use. The 7.2 kW rating is the most the system can produce at one moment in ideal conditions. In practice, it produces less than that most of the time, depending on the time of day, the season, and the weather.

## A typical day's production

On a sunny day, the 7.2 kW rooftop solar system produces about 35 kWh in total. Production starts around 6 AM, climbs through the morning, and peaks around noon at about 5 kWh per hour. It then falls through the afternoon and ends around 7 PM. Most of the day's energy arrives between 9 AM and 4 PM. That midday surplus is what charges your battery for the evening, when the 4 PM to 9 PM peak prices begin.

## Cloudy days

On a cloudy day, the rooftop solar system produces roughly a third of what it makes on a sunny day, around 10 kWh instead of 35 kWh, with a midday peak of about 1.5 kWh per hour. That is often not enough to cover the home's daytime use and fill the 13.5 kWh battery. A low solar forecast for tomorrow is the main reason to charge the battery from the grid overnight, during the cheapest hours.

## How solar and the battery work together

Your rooftop solar system and VoltNest 13 battery share the work in a set order. Solar power goes first to whatever your home is using right now. Any extra solar then charges the battery. Once the battery is full, the rest is exported to the grid for a credit. In the evening, when solar production stops, the battery powers your home with the energy it stored during the day, which keeps you from buying power at peak prices.

## Keeping panels productive

Rooftop solar panels need very little care. Dust and pollen can lower production by a few percent, and rain usually washes the panels clean on its own. If production looks much lower than usual on a sunny day, check the inverter for warning lights in the app. Never climb onto the roof to clean or inspect the panels yourself. Any work on the panels, roof wiring, or inverter must be done by a licensed electrician or a certified solar technician.
